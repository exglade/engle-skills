# What

Teach one concept clearly. Leave the call on whether it fits the user's project or situation to them.

## Steps

1. **Pin the concept.** If the term is ambiguous, say which meaning you're explaining.
2. **Gauge the level** from what the user said. If you can't tell, aim at a smart adult new to the topic.
3. **Check primary sources** (official docs, specs, the original paper or author) for specifics; memory drifts on
   details.
4. **Write the explanation** in the shape below. Done when every section that applies is filled.

## Shape

- **In one line:** what it is.
- **The problem it solves:** why it exists.
- **How it works:** the core idea, then a small concrete example. Add a diagram if it carries the idea faster.
- **When it helps** and **when it hurts:** only when it's something people choose or apply; skip both for facts of
  nature or history.
- **Often confused with:** nearby terms, and the difference.
- **Sources:** two or three links worth reading next.

For "X vs Y", compare them side by side in a table, then say what decides between them.

## Style

- Plain language, short sentences, in the user's language. Define each term the first time you use it.
- Concrete before abstract.
- Mark anything uncertain or disputed.
